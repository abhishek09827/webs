# Music Art Direction Skill

## 1. Deconstruction of Audio Reference Material
When analyzing artist cover art, music videos, and hardware gear:
- **Frequency-to-Color Mapping:**
  - Sub-bass (<80Hz): Dense, deep voids (#050505, #0a0a0a, pitch charcoal).
  - Transients & Clipping: Piercing, high-contrast shock colors (Crimson Red `#FF0033`, Pure Blood `#E6002B`, Electric White `#FFFFFF`).
  - Metadata / Calibration: Functional mono contrast (#737373, #262626).
- **Physical Hardware Textures:**
  - Akai MPC pads, Roland TR-808 matte chassis, flight-case aluminum rivets, VU meters, LED peak clips, phosphor CRT displays.
  - Subtly layered film grain (opacity ~0.04-0.08) and 1px scanlines preventing flat digital gradients.

## 2. Typography Hierarchy for Music Production Sites
- **Display (The Hero Identity):** Wide, geometric, aggressive, or technical display typeface (e.g., Syne, Clash Display, Monument Extended, or aggressive custom typographic treatments).
- **Metadata (DAW / Stems / Transport):** High-density Monospace (e.g., JetBrains Mono, Space Mono, IBM Plex Mono) formatted with musical notation:
  - `BPM: 142.0`
  - `KEY: C# MIN`
  - `BAR: 04.2.1`
  - `LUFS: -7.8`
  - `CH: 01-DRUMS [ACTIVE]`

## 3. Motion Language Formulation
- **The DAW Arrangement Metaphor:** Elements do not float randomly. They sit on horizontal timelines, rack strips, or 16-step grids.
- **Snapping & Quantization:** Micro-interactions mimic hardware buttons: snappy attack (instant down), exponential decay (`power2.out` or `expo.out`), and zero gummy "floatiness."
- **Audio Reactivity:** Peak meters react instantaneously to kick/bass hits, decaying over ~180ms.
